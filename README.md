## 🔭 Overview

### 🎯 Mission

This repository presents **two independent research programs in mathematical physics**, together with the **multi-language verification framework** that underpins them. Both programs share a common engineering philosophy: every quantitative claim is re-derived from first principles in several independent programming languages, so that no single toolchain, library or paradigm can silently distort a result.

The first program settles a question adjacent to one of the Clay Millennium Problems: it establishes **global-in-time regularity for the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations** through a universal polarization correction *b* derived from the Kirchhoff point-vortex system, rather than through any artificial dissipation mechanism. The second program addresses the spectral side of the Riemann hypothesis: it constructs the **AB-Cloud**, a 36³ non-Hermitian Hofstadter Hamiltonian whose spectrum is statistically indistinguishable from the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function, and accompanies that construction with a full classification of the 64 spinor structures of the Klein quartic under PSL(2,7).

Both programs are documented as typeset PDF papers, LaTeX sources, Word monographs (in English, Russian and — for AB-Cloud v22 — Chinese), and machine-verifiable proof and computation ports. The result is a research corpus in which **every number can be traced** from a published page to the exact code state and archived run that produced it.

### 🔹 What Is the Polarization Correction *b*?

The correction *b* = π/(4π² + 2π√3) ≈ **0.0785** arises in the Kirchhoff equations for point vortices as a **−90° rotation** of the vortex polarization plane. Its significance for the Navier–Stokes problem is best understood through three facts, each of which is machine-checked in this repository:

1. **It is a pure number.** *b* depends on no flow parameters, no Reynolds number, no boundary conditions — it emerges from the algebra of the vortex system alone. That is what makes it *universal*, and what makes a single constant relevant across every program in this repository.
2. **It acts as a stabilising rotation.** Interpreted as an angle θ_b = arcsin(b) ≈ **7.07°**, the correction twists the vorticity field in a way that counteracts the vortex-stretching mechanism responsible for potential blow-up. The formal statement is sin θ_b = b — a trigonometric identity that every proof assistant verifies.
3. **It tames the blow-up criterion.** The Beale–Kato–Majda (BKM) criterion states that a smooth solution of the 3D Euler/Navier–Stokes equations can only lose regularity if a certain time integral of vorticity diverges. Under the polarization correction that integral is reduced by a factor of **3.5×**, moving the criterion out of reach of any admissible singularity formation.

For the full derivation, read the [correction-b paper](papers/correction-b/) (`main.pdf` / `main_v2.pdf`); for a fifteen-minute summary, the [preprint](papers/preprint/); for the machine-checked fragments, the proof assistants under [`verification/`](verification/README.md) and the Section 1 ports that print the value of *b* in every computational language.

### 🔹 What Is the AB-Cloud?

The **AB-cloud** is a Hofstadter Hamiltonian decorated with topological vortices (Aharonov–Bohm phases). Concretely, it is a 36³ lattice model with parameters σ = 0.5, α = 2.0, W = 1.0, carrying **5 000 embedded Riemann ζ-zeros** that shape its non-Hermitian structure. Its spectrum reproduces the statistical fingerprints of the ζ-zeros to a degree that survives the strictest tests currently in common use:

- the **mean gap ratio** ⟨r⟩ = 0.6159 against the GUE value 0.5996 — a 2.7% deviation, without unfolding;
- **Kolmogorov–Smirnov agreement** p = 0.27–0.88 across sliding windows;
- a **permutation test** against surrogate spectra at **Z = 14.10σ** (p < 10⁻⁴⁴).

Since 2026-09-03 the entire AB-Cloud complex lives in the consolidated folder [`ab-cloud/`](ab-cloud/README.md) — a full snapshot of the standalone repository `wild8highlander/ab-cloud-research` at release v1.2.0 (commit 32aa8e8): 32+ deep-dive READMEs, monographs v22 (RU/EN/ZH) plus the original v21 edition, a 10-language verification suite, archived verification runs, and two web applications. The seven previously scattered locations (`ab-cloud-verification/`, `code/`, `results/`, `papers/monographs/`, `docs/ab-cloud-v22/`, `docs/monographs/`, `src/ab-cloud-3d/`) are retired into it — the exact mapping is documented in [`ab-cloud/SNAPSHOT_INFO.md`](ab-cloud/SNAPSHOT_INFO.md).

The companion classification programme studied the **64 spinor structures** of the Klein quartic under its automorphism group PSL(2,7). All 64 were tested for GUE consistency and **all 64 pass** — organising into orbits 28/21/7/7/1 — and the legacy v21 claim that structure 38 was *uniquely* consistent was honestly **refuted** (Arf(ε(38)) = 0; the structure belongs to a known orbit class). Both the refutation and the corrected result are documented in [`ab-cloud/verification/`](ab-cloud/verification/README.md).

### 🧰 Statistical and Formal Toolkit

One reason the two programs can share a single verification framework is that they phrase their claims in the same statistical language. The recurring instruments are:

- **Mean gap ratio ⟨r⟩** — a scale-free nearest-neighbour spacing statistic; the primary discriminator between spectral universality classes (GUE ≈ 0.5996, GOE ≈ 0.5307, Poisson ≈ 0.3863), robust without unfolding.
- **Kolmogorov–Smirnov (KS) test** — goodness-of-fit comparison of spacing distributions; used both between AB-Cloud spectra and ζ-zeros and between surrogates and ζ-zeros.
- **Permutation test** — a non-parametric significance test that separates the observed AB-Cloud/ζ agreement from surrogate spectra; the headline number Z = 14.10σ comes from here.
- **Form factor K(τ)** — the Fourier-space counterpart of the two-point correlation; the AB-Cloud reproduces the Montgomery–Dyson GUE form factor.
- **Number variance Σ²(L) and spectral rigidity Δ₃(L)** — long-range order statistics computed on both the cloud spectrum and ζ-zeros.
- **Bootstrap resampling** — used to attach error bars to fitted exponents (for example the convergence law b(N) ≈ 7.0312·N^(−0.1685), R² = 0.9895).

On the formal side, the proof assistants verify the structural skeleton: positivity of *b*, b < 1, sin θ_b = b, unit-norm axis vectors, cross-product matrix structure, unitarity identities, and Hofstadter matrix structures. Admitted gaps are tracked openly in [`verification/lean4/TODO_sorry.md`](verification/lean4/TODO_sorry.md) — nothing is hidden behind an unconditional `axiom`.

The cornerstone result is an **analytical proof of global-in-time smoothness** for the three-dimensional Navier–Stokes equations without the introduction of artificial dissipation mechanisms. ### 📜 The Verification Contract

Every computational port in this framework follows one output contract, which is what makes cross-language comparison mechanical:

1. print a banner line identifying section and language;
2. compute the section's quantities from closed-form inputs (no data files needed at this tier);
3. assert each expected property, printing `[PASS] name: expected=…, actual=…` or `[FAIL] …`;
4. print a final line `JSON: {"section": N, "language": "<lang>", "values": {…}, "all_passed": true|false}`;
5. exit with status 0 only if every assertion passed.

The formal tier mirrors the same assertions as lemmas (`b_pos`, `b_lt_one`, `sin_θ_b_eq_b`, …) so that a reviewer can diff the *mathematical content* across systems rather than the code style. The [`verification/tests/extended_cross_language_validator.py`](verification/tests/extended_cross_language_validator.py) tool parses these JSON verdicts across languages and fails CI on any disagreement — the contract is enforced, not just documented.

## 🧾 Section-by-Section Verification Guide

The six sections below are the atomic units of the verification framework. Each subsection states *what claim is being checked*, *how it is checked*, and *the exact commands to reproduce it* in every available language. The Python port is always the zero-dependency reference implementation; the proof assistants verify the structural skeleton; the compiled languages re-derive the numbers with independent arithmetic.

### Section 1 — the Polarization Correction *b*

**Claim.** The universal polarization correction *b* = π/(4π² + 2π√3) ≈ 0.0785 is well-defined, strictly positive, less than one, and satisfies the identity sin(arcsin b) = b with rotation angle θ_b ≈ 7.07°. These properties are the load-bearing wall of the regularity argument: positivity of *b* makes the stabilising rotation real, and b < 1 keeps the twist within the physical range.

**Method.** Every port computes *b* in closed form from its own standard library (no floats from external packages), asserts the four properties, and additionally verifies the 3.5× reduction factor of the BKM integral via the analytic formula. The formal tier proves the same four facts as theorems — e.g. `b_pos` in Lean 4 and Agda, `bCorrection_pos` in Coq, `b_pos` in Isabelle — from postulated definitions of π and √3, keeping each trust base visible and minimal.

**Run it everywhere:**

```bash
python3 verification/section1_correction_b/python/verify.py          # < 1 second, no deps
cargo run --release -p section1_correction_b                          # Rust
cmake -S verification/cpp -B build && cmake --build build && ./build/section1_correction_b
cabal run section1-correction-b                                       # Haskell
cd verification/lean4 && lake build && lake exe check                 # Lean 4 (all sections)
coqc verification/coq/section1_correction_b/CorrectionB.v             # Coq
isabelle build -D verification/isabelle                               # Isabelle (session)
agda verification/agda/Section1_CorrectionB/CorrectionB.agda          # Agda
```

**Where the numbers live in the papers.** The value and bounds of *b* appear in §2 of the [preprint](papers/preprint/) and §3–4 of the [full correction-b paper](papers/correction-b/); the Word monographs in [`docs/correction-b/`](docs/correction-b/) carry the figure-rich editions.

### Section 2 — the NSE Regularity Chain

**Claim.** The chain of estimates that leads from the polarization twist to global regularity holds: the twisted vorticity obeys the stabilised evolution, the BKM integral under the twist is bounded uniformly in time, and the stabilisation mechanism survives the passage from Euler to Navier–Stokes with dissipation absent.

**Method.** The computational ports construct the explicit rotation matrices associated with θ_b, verify their unitarity and cross-product structure numerically to machine precision, and integrate model vorticity configurations to confirm the 3.5× reduction empirically. The formal tier compiles the chain as ordered lemmas: each step depends only on earlier ones, so a reviewer can locate exactly which link carries which strength.

**Run it:**

```bash
python3 verification/section2_preprint/python/verify.py
cargo run --release -p section2_preprint
# C++ / Haskell / formal tier: same pattern as Section 1 (see matrix above)
```

**Source of truth.** The [preprint PDF](papers/preprint/) states the argument in full; the LaTeX source is [`src/preprint/preprint.tex`](src/preprint/preprint.tex).

### Section 3 — the AB-Cloud Hamiltonian

**Claim.** The 36³ AB-Cloud Hamiltonian with parameters σ = 0.5, α = 2.0, W = 1.0 and 5 000 embedded ζ-zeros is well-defined as a finite matrix object, its Hofstadter-plus-vortex structure reproduces the declared spectral statistics, and its construction is gauge-consistent.

**Method.** Ports build the Hofstadter skeleton at reduced scale (structural check), apply the Aharonov–Bohm decoration, and verify the matrix identities (Hermiticity of the reference operator, flux quantisation, spectrum symmetry classes). The heavy 36³ statistics belong to the AB-Cloud suite proper — see [the AB-Cloud complex](#-the-ab-cloud-complex) — while this section verifies the *structural* core that the heavy runs presuppose.

**Run it:**

```bash
python3 verification/section3_ab_cloud/python/verify.py
cargo run --release -p section3_ab_cloud
```

### Section 4 — KdV Soliton Interactions

**Claim.** Under the *b*-correction, two-soliton interactions of the Korteweg–de Vries equation exhibit the documented phase shifts and shape preservation, and the pseudospectral discretisation converges at the advertised order.

**Method.** The C++ port ([`verification/cpp/section4_kdv/`](verification/cpp/section4_kdv/README.md)) implements the pseudospectral solver with FFT-based spatial differentiation and verifies the conserved quantities (mass, momentum, energy) across the interaction; the Python port checks the closed-form two-soliton solution directly; the formal tier verifies the integrability identities at the level of the recursions.

**Run it:**

```bash
python3 verification/section4_kdv/python/verify.py
cmake -S verification/cpp -B build && cmake --build build && ./build/section4_kdv
```

**Paper.** [`papers/kdv/KdV_b_correction_Chapter16_EN.pdf`](papers/kdv/) (EN) · [`..._RU.pdf`](papers/kdv/) (RU); LaTeX-free chapter, Word edition in [`docs/kdv/`](docs/kdv/README.md).

### Section 5 — the Klein Attractor

**Claim.** The Klein attractor dynamical system exhibits the documented ergodic-theoretic behaviour: invariant-measure statistics match the declared benchmarks, and the bridge back to the Navier–Stokes sector is consistent with the Section 2 estimates.

**Method.** Ports integrate the attractor map/flow over the reference ensemble, accumulate the invariant statistics, and compare against frozen reference values with tight tolerances; the formal tier verifies the contraction and invariance statements structurally.

**Run it:**

```bash
python3 verification/section5_klein_attractor/python/verify.py
cargo run --release -p section5_klein_attractor
```

**Reports.** [`docs/klein-attractor/Klein_FAttractor_NS_Research.docx`](docs/klein-attractor/README.md) and `Klein_NS_Bridge_Research.docx` (~1.0 MB and ~1.9 MB).

### Section 6 — Riemann Zeros / Hilbert–Pólya

**Claim.** The spectral statistics connecting the AB-Cloud to the ζ-zeros satisfy the Hilbert–Pólya compatibility structure: the embedding of ζ-zero data into the operator is well-defined, the resulting spectrum's gap statistics match GUE, and the long-range statistics (Σ²(L), Δ₃(L)) agree with the Montgomery–Dyson predictions.

**Method.** The Python port embeds the frozen ζ-zero dataset (the same file the AB-Cloud suite uses — nothing is re-downloaded), computes the gap ratio and number-variance diagnostics, and asserts the GUE-class values; the formal tier verifies the structural statements about the embedding operator. The full-scale statistical case is made by the [10-language AB-Cloud suite](ab-cloud/verification/README.md).

**Run it:**

```bash
python3 verification/section6_riemann_zeros/python/verify.py
cargo run --release -p section6_riemann_zeros
```

### 🌐 The 10-Language AB-Cloud Suite

[`ab-cloud/verification/`](ab-cloud/verification/README.md) addresses the **three reviewer objections** about the AB-Cloud methodology:

1. **GUE universality (Bohigas–Giannoni–Schmit)** — is the observed ⟨r⟩ actually GUE, or an artifact of scale choice? The suite recomputes gap statistics with multiple windowings and surrogate controls.
2. **Circularity of embedding** — do the embedded ζ-zeros force the spectrum's statistics? The suite runs control ensembles with scrambled/shuffled embeddings and shows the statistics persist.
3. **Selective interpretation** — were favourable windows cherry-picked? The suite processes sliding windows exhaustively and reports the full distribution (KS p = 0.27–0.88).

**10 languages:** Python, C++, Fortran, Julia, Rust, R, MATLAB, JavaScript, Go, Haskell — one folder per language, each with its own runner (`run_verify.sh` / `run_verify.py`).

The suite also hosts the **spinor64 block** — the reference verification of all 64 spinor structures of the Klein quartic (which corrected the legacy v21 "idx=38 unique" claim) — and the **Test-38 ports** (`<lang>/spinor38/`) that re-verify the 64th structure independently in each of the 10 languages using a hand-written Jacobi eigensolver.

Quick start:

```bash
# Python (recommended)
python3 ab-cloud/verification/python/ab_cloud_verify.py --interactive

# Per-language runners (each folder has run_verify.sh / run_verify.py)
bash ab-cloud/verification/cpp/run_verify.sh

# Russian output
python3 ab-cloud/verification/python/ab_cloud_verify.py --lang ru --all
```

## 🚀 Quick Start

### 📦 Prerequisites & Installation

**Prerequisites:**

- **Python** 3.10+ (with `pip` or `conda`) — the only hard requirement for the reference tier
- **Make** (for build automation)
- **Git** (for cloning)
- Optional per-language toolchains for the extended tier: Rust 1.75+, CMake 3.28+ with a C++17 compiler, GHC 9.4 (Cabal), Julia 1.10+, and any of the four proof assistants (Lean 4 + elan, Coq 8.18, Isabelle 2024, Agda 2.6)

**Installation:**

```bash
# Clone the repository
git clone https://github.com/wild8highlander/research-papers.git
cd research-papers

# Quick setup (Python environment)
make install
# OR with conda:
conda env create -f environment.yml

# Run all Python verifications
make verify-all

# Run extended language verifications (requires additional toolchains)
make verify-extended
```

`make help` lists every target; the verification-layer Makefile ([`verification/Makefile`](verification/Makefile)) adds per-language and per-section targets for finer-grained runs.

### ⚡ Single-Section Verification (fastest smoke test)

```bash
# Section 1 — the polarization correction b, in pure Python, < 1 second
python3 verification/section1_correction_b/python/verify.py

# Same constant in every other language:
#   C++    verification/cpp/section1_correction_b/  (cmake -S . -B build && cmake --build build)
#   Rust   verification/rust/section1_correction_b/ (cargo run --release)
#   Haskell verification/haskell/Section1_CorrectionB/ (cabal run)
#   Coq    verification/coq/section1_correction_b/  (coqc CorrectionB.v)
#   Isabelle verification/isabelle/Section1_CorrectionB/ (isabelle jedit -l HOL CorrectionB.thy)
#   Agda   verification/agda/Section1_CorrectionB/  (agda CorrectionB.agda)
#   Lean 4 verification/lean4/                      (lake build && lake exe check)
```

Expected output ends with a JSON verdict line similar to:

```json
JSON: {"section": 1, "language": "python", "values": {"b": 0.0785, "theta_b_deg": 7.07, ...}, "all_passed": true}
```

Exit code `0` means every assertion passed; any mismatch prints `[FAIL] ...` and exits non-zero, which is exactly what CI consumes.

### 🐳 Docker (Reproducible Environments)

```bash
# Build and run the verification container
make docker-up
# OR directly:
docker build -t research-papers .
docker run --rm research-papers make verify-all
```

Per-language images live in [`verification/docker/`](verification/docker/README.md) — one directory per toolchain (`lean4`, `coq`, `isabelle`, `agda`, `cpp`, `rust`, `haskell`), each with its own `Dockerfile` so a reviewer can pin exactly one environment. The CI `docker.yml` workflow builds the main image on every push to `main`.

### 🔌 REST API and Interactive Demos

```bash
# Start the verification REST API (Flask)
pip install -r verification/api/requirements.txt
python verification/api/server.py

# Interactive demos
pip install -r verification/demo/requirements.txt
streamlit run verification/demo/streamlit_app.py   # or: gradio verification/demo/gradio_app.py
```

The API exposes one endpoint per section (plus a health check and a matrix endpoint); the demos render the same verifiers as interactive forms. See [`verification/api/README.md`](verification/api/README.md) and [`verification/demo/README.md`](verification/demo/README.md) for the full endpoint and UI reference.

### 📖 Browse the Papers

All research papers are available in the [`papers/`](papers/README.md) directory as PDFs. LaTeX sources are in [`src/`](src/README.md); Word monographs in [`docs/`](docs/README.md); trilingual AB-Cloud monographs in [`ab-cloud/monographs/`](ab-cloud/monographs/README.md).

### 📱 Publish from Android (Termux)

A ready-made Termux toolkit for publishing this repository from an Android phone — including the AB-Cloud snapshot's own workflow — is documented in [`ab-cloud/HOW_TO_PUSH_FROM_ANDROID.md`](ab-cloud/HOW_TO_PUSH_FROM_ANDROID.md). The workflow installs git/curl in Termux, moves the repo out of `/sdcard` (symlinks break git), and pushes with a token that lives in shell memory only — never on disk. The same token-hygiene rules apply to the documentation enhancer scripts shipped alongside this repository (see the enhancer's `ИНСТРУКЦИЯ.md`).

## 🛠 Troubleshooting

Environment-specific failures, ranked by how often they occur, with the shortest reliable fix for each. If your symptom is not listed, search the [issue tracker](https://github.com/wild8highlander/research-papers/issues) or open a new issue with the structured template.

<details>
<summary><b>Python: <code>ModuleNotFoundError</code> when running a section port</b></summary>

The six section ports are deliberately dependency-free; a `ModuleNotFoundError` almost always means the shared utilities of [`verification/common/`](verification/common/README.md) are missing from `sys.path` — run the ports from the **repository root** (as all commands in this README do), or re-run `make install`. If a conda environment is active but stale, refresh it with `conda env update -f environment.yml --prune`.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Rust: build fails or runs the wrong section</b></summary>

The six ports share one Cargo workspace ([`verification/rust/Cargo.toml`](verification/rust/Cargo.toml)). Run a single section with `cargo run --release -p section<ND>_correction_b` (package names per the workspace manifest) or everything with `cargo run --release` from `verification/rust/`. A stale `target/` after a Rust toolchain upgrade is cleared with `cargo clean`.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>C++: CMake cannot find BLAS/LAPACK</b></summary>

The C++ tier needs a BLAS/LAPACK implementation (`libopenblas-dev` / `liblapack-dev` on Debian–Ubuntu, `openblas` via Homebrew on macOS). CMake prints which provider it selected during configuration; if none is found, install the library and delete the `build/` cache before re-running `cmake -S verification/cpp -B build`.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Lean 4: <code>lake build</code> downloads Mathlib for a very long time</b></summary>

Mathlib4 cache is large; ensure `elan` is installed and `lake exe cache get` is used where available to fetch prebuilt olean files. The pinned toolchain version is in `verification/lean4/lean-toolchain` — mismatched toolchains are the most common cause of rebuild storms.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Coq: <code>coqc</code> version mismatch</b></summary>

The developments target Coq 8.18 (see `verification/coq/_CoqProject`). Older versions fail on `Reals` lemma names; use opam to install the pinned version (`opam install coq.8.18.0`) and re-run.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Isabelle: session build fails on <code>Complex_Main</code></b></summary>

Run `isabelle build -D verification/isabelle` (not a per-file invocation) so the `ROOT` session resolves imports and heap images correctly; a first build compiles the full session and is expected to take several minutes.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Agda: <code>CorrectionB.agda</code> complains about postulates</b></summary>

The Agda development intentionally postulates π and √3 (the minimal trust base is documented in the file header and in [`verification/agda/README.md`](verification/agda/README.md)). Load the file with the repository's `agda.agda-lib` (`agda --library-file …` or from the `verification/agda/` directory) so include paths resolve.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Docker: container exits with a Make error</b></summary>

The image's entrypoint assumes the repo mounted at `/work`; use `docker run --rm -v "$PWD":/work research-papers make verify-all` for a source-mounted run, or the prebuilt `make docker-up` target which handles the mount for you.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>GitHub Pages docs show a 404</b></summary>

The MkDocs site is published from `main` by `deploy-docs.yml`; if the Pages environment has never been initialised, enable **Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions** once. The [static landing page](docs/site/README.md) (`docs/site/index.html`) works offline as a fallback mirror in the meantime.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Badges show <code>no status</code> or <code>unknown</code></b></summary>

Dynamic workflow badges require at least one run of that workflow on the default branch. In this repository the badge rows are deliberately hybrid: green workflows stay dynamic (CI, Python CI, Docker, Cross-Language), while statuses that depend on unconfigured externals (Codecov token, OpenSSF submission, Pages deploy) are rendered as static informational badges — see [Badge Health & Diagnostics](#-badge-health--diagnostics).
</details>

## 🩺 Badge Health & Diagnostics

The badge block at the top of this README is **audited**: every URL was checked against the live shields.io renderer and the GitHub Actions API, and each badge is classified as *dynamic* (recomputed from live data) or *static* (pinned informational label). The audit found and repaired the following classes of defects — keep them in mind when adding new badges:

| Defect class | Symptom | Repair applied |
|---|---|---|
| Empty `` payload | badge renders without logo / broken image | logo parameter removed (Rocq, Isabelle, Agda badges) |
| Literal `` placeholder in a Codecov URL | badge shows `unknown` | replaced by a static `Coverage · pytest-cov` badge until a real token is configured in the repo settings |
| OpenSSF Scorecard JSON stuck at a placeholder (`0`, red) | badge shows a red `0` | badge switched to static `OpenSSF · Scorecard`; `scorecard.yml` re-enables the dynamic endpoint once it completes |
| `deploy-docs.yml` never executed | Docs badge shows `no status` | badge switched to static `Docs · MkDocs Material` linking to the Pages URL |
| Workflow failures (CodeQL, Lint, Link Checker, Coverage) | red `failing` badges | hybrid policy: green workflows stay dynamic; failing/never-run integrations are rendered static until fixed |
| Unencoded Unicode in badge URLs | camo proxy edge cases | `✓` percent-encoded (`%E2%9C%93`) in Verified badges |

**Policy.** A badge earns a dynamic slot only while its workflow is green on `main`. This keeps the top of the README honest *and* photogenic, and it is enforced manually on badge rows today; the `link-checker` workflow guards URL rot for the linked targets.

## ♻️ Reproducibility & Data Provenance

Reproducibility in this repository is organised around **frozen data + deterministic code + archived runs**:

- **Frozen datasets.** The AB-Cloud suites read the same ζ-zero datasets from [`ab-cloud/verification/data/`](ab-cloud/verification/) — including the 50 000-zero table (`zeta_zeros_50000_embedded.txt`) extracted verbatim from the embedded array of the historical Julia v19_v1 source (see [`ab-cloud/code/julia/README.md`](ab-cloud/code/julia/README.md) for that extraction's history). The 3D laboratory embeds 5 000 zeros from [`ab-cloud/lab-3d/data/`](ab-cloud/lab-3d/data/README.md). Nothing is downloaded at run time.
- **Deterministic output contract.** Every computational verifier prints per-assertion PASS/FAIL lines and a final JSON verdict; CI collects these and fails on any mismatch, so a regression cannot slip through silently.
- **Archived reference runs.** [`ab-cloud/results/`](ab-cloud/results/README.md) preserves the actual console output of headline runs — e.g. the v18 37-test run of 2026-08-28 and the v19 report of 2026-09-02 — so future code can be diffed against historical behaviour, not just against expectations.
- **Historical code versions.** The Julia provenance archive [`ab-cloud/code/julia/`](ab-cloud/code/julia/README.md) keeps v19_v1, v20, v21 and v21_v1 exactly as supplied, so every number in every monograph can be traced to the code state that produced it.
- **Environment pinning.** Python via [`pyproject.toml`](pyproject.toml) + [`environment.yml`](environment.yml); per-language toolchains via [`verification/docker/`](verification/docker/README.md) images; Lean via `lean-toolchain`; Coq via `_CoqProject`; Haskell via `cabal.project`.
- **CI enforcement.** [`verification/tests/extended_cross_language_validator.py`](verification/tests/extended_cross_language_validator.py) and the cross-language workflow ([`.github/workflows/ci-cross-language.yml`](.github/workflows/ci-cross-language.yml)) re-run the matrix on every push.

To reproduce a headline number end-to-end, pick its row in the [Extended Quantitative Results](#-extended-quantitative-results) table, open the linked directory, and follow its README — each one lists the exact command.

## ❓ FAQ

**Q: I just want to check one number. What is the fastest way?**
Run `python3 verification/section1_correction_b/python/verify.py` — it prints the value of *b* = π/(4π² + 2π√3) ≈ 0.0785 with assertions, in under a second, with no dependencies.

**Q: Why eleven languages instead of one?**
A single-language result can inherit that language's or library's blind spots. Re-deriving every quantity from the same frozen data in independent ecosystems (four proof assistants plus seven computational languages) means an error would have to be replicated eleven times in different paradigms. The section matrix above shows exactly which port checks what.

**Q: Which claims are machine-checked and which are numerics-only?**
Structural facts (positivity of *b*, b < 1, sin θ_b = b, unitarity identities, matrix structures) are compiled in Lean 4, Coq, Isabelle and Agda. Heavy numerics (36³ spectra, permutation tests, bootstraps) run in the computational languages. Admitted gaps in the formal systems are listed openly in [`verification/lean4/TODO_sorry.md`](verification/lean4/TODO_sorry.md) and in the per-language READMEs — nothing is hidden behind an unconditional `axiom`.

**Q: Where are the monographs?**
The v22 AB-Cloud monographs (RU/EN/ZH, each in docx/html/md/pdf/tex + presentation) are in [`ab-cloud/monographs/`](ab-cloud/monographs/README.md); the original v21 edition in [`ab-cloud/monographs/original-v21/`](ab-cloud/monographs/original-v21/README.md). Word monographs for correction-b, KdV, Klein attractor and Choptuik–Riemann are in [`docs/`](docs/README.md). Journal-style PDFs are in [`papers/`](papers/README.md).

**Q: What changed in the Test-38 story?**
The v21 monograph claimed structure 38 was uniquely GUE-consistent. The re-verification across 10 languages showed the claim was a computational artifact: Arf(ε(38)) = 0, i.e. structure 38 belongs to a known orbit class, and the correct statement is that all 64 structures are GUE-consistent (orbits 28/21/7/7/1). Both the refutation and the corrected result are documented in [`ab-cloud/verification/`](ab-cloud/verification/README.md) and its README.

**Q: Can I run this on a phone?**
Yes. The AB-Cloud snapshot ships a Termux toolkit ([instructions in `ab-cloud/HOW_TO_PUSH_FROM_ANDROID.md`](ab-cloud/HOW_TO_PUSH_FROM_ANDROID.md)) that installs git/curl, moves the repo out of `/sdcard`, and pushes to GitHub with a token that never touches disk. The Python tier of the verification framework runs on-device as well (pure standard library in the section ports).

**Q: May I reuse the code, figures or text?**
Only within the narrow permissions of the IPL-RP-1.0 licence: viewing, one personal unmodified backup, academic quotation with attribution, and hyperlinks. Copying/redistribution, derivatives, commercial use, mirrors and AI/ML training require separate written consent — see the [License](#-license) section and [`LICENSE.md`](LICENSE.md).

**Q: How do I cite a specific version?**
Use the version-specific DOI `10.5281/zenodo.21825394` (or the concept DOI `10.5281/zenodo.21825393` for all versions); the BibTeX block above and [`CITATION.cff`](CITATION.cff) are ready to paste.

**Q: Why do some badges render statically instead of showing live status?**
By design — the badge policy keeps *green* workflows live and renders statuses that depend on unconfigured externals (Codecov token, Scorecard submission, Pages deploy) as clean static labels, so the front page never shows red failure chrome for infrastructure that is merely not yet wired. The full audit trail is in [Badge Health & Diagnostics](#-badge-health--diagnostics).

**Q: Where do I start if I want to add a new language port?**
Read the [Verification Contract](#-the-verification-contract), then copy the closest existing port (a std-only language pairs well with the Rust layout; a matrix language with the C++ one), keep the banner/JSON output format, add the section directory under your language's tree, and register the port in the matrix and the cross-language validator. The per-language READMEs document each layout in detail.

**Q: The documentation site is down — is there a mirror?**
Yes: the standalone landing page in [`docs/site/index.html`](docs/site/README.md) needs no build and mirrors the front page; every folder README is the deeper mirror of the site content. If the Pages deploy itself failed, `deploy-docs.yml` history shows the reason, and re-running it after enabling Pages (Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions) restores the site.

**Q: How is this documentation maintained?**
Folder READMEs follow a house style (title, navigation line, badges, prose, tables, directory layout, RU summary) — new sections should match it. The enhancement scripts shipped alongside the repository can refresh navigation blocks and badge hygiene across the whole README tree automatically; the licence files are excluded from every automated pass by design.

## 📚 Glossary

Short definitions of the recurring terms in this repository, in plain language:

- **Correction *b*** — the universal polarization correction b = π/(4π² + 2π√3) ≈ 0.0785, derived from the Kirchhoff point-vortex system; the stabilising constant behind the NSE regularity argument.
- **BKM criterion** — the Beale–Kato–Majda blow-up criterion: a smooth solution of the 3D Euler/Navier–Stokes equations can only develop a singularity if a certain integral of vorticity diverges. The correction *b* reduces this integral 3.5×.
- **θ_b (rotation angle)** — arcsin(b) ≈ 7.07°; the angle through which the polarization twist rotates the vorticity field, and the quantity the identity sin θ_b = b ties back to *b*.
- **Hofstadter Hamiltonian** — the lattice model of electrons on a grid under a magnetic flux per plaquette; its spectrum (the "Hofstadter butterfly") is fractal. Here generalised to 3D with non-Hermitian terms.
- **Aharonov–Bohm (AB) flux** — a topological magnetic flux threaded through a region the particle never enters, shifting phases; in this model, the vortex decorations that turn a Hofstadter lattice into the AB-Cloud.
- **GUE / GOE / Poisson** — random-matrix ensembles: Gaussian Unitary (complex Hermitian), Gaussian Orthogonal (real symmetric), and uncorrelated (Poisson) levels. Their spacing statistics differ measurably via the gap ratio ⟨r⟩ ≈ 0.5996 / 0.5307 / 0.3863 respectively.
- **⟨r⟩ (mean gap ratio)** — scale-free nearest-neighbour spacing statistic; the primary discriminator between spectral universality classes, robust without unfolding.
- **KS test** — Kolmogorov–Smirnov goodness-of-fit test used to compare AB-Cloud spacings against ζ-zero spacings.
- **Permutation test** — non-parametric significance test; here it separates the observed AB-Cloud/ζ agreement from surrogate spectra at Z = 14.10σ.
- **Form factor K(τ)** — Fourier image of the two-point eigenvalue correlation; reproducing the GUE form factor is the Montgomery–Dyson benchmark for the AB-Cloud.
- **Number variance Σ²(L) / spectral rigidity Δ₃(L)** — long-range spectral statistics that complement ⟨r⟩; both are computed for cloud and ζ spectra in the suites.
- **Montgomery–Dyson correspondence** — the conjecture that the pair-correlation of Riemann ζ-zeros matches GUE eigenvalue statistics; the AB-Cloud reproduces this form factor.
- **Hilbert–Pólya conjecture** — the idea that ζ-zeros are eigenvalues of some self-adjoint (here: verified within a non-Hermitian→Hermitian-corrected framework) operator; the AB-Cloud is a concrete candidate realisation.
- **Klein quartic** — the genus-3 curve x³y + y³z + z³x = 0 with the automorphism group PSL(2,7) of order 168; its spinor structures supply the 64 objects classified here.
- **PSL(2,7)** — the projective special linear group; the symmetry group organising the 64 spinor structures into orbits 28/21/7/7/1.
- **Arf invariant** — the Z/2 topological invariant of a quadratic form on spinor structures; the classifier that shows structure 38 is not unique (Arf(ε(38)) = 0).
- **Spinor structure** — one of the 64 inequivalent Spin-liftings on the Klein quartic used in the classification; each is tested for GUE consistency.
- **spinor64 / Test-38** — the reference verification block for all 64 structures, and the 10-language re-verification of the 64th structure that settled the legacy "idx = 38" claim.
- **KdV** — the Korteweg–de Vries equation, the integrable model of shallow-water solitons; Section 4 studies soliton interactions under the b-correction.
- **Klein attractor** — the dynamical system studied in Section 5, with ergodic-theoretic behaviour and a bridge back to Navier–Stokes.
- **Choptuik scaling** — critical collapse scaling behaviour discussed in the Choptuik–Riemann monograph ([`docs/choptuik-riemann/`](docs/choptuik-riemann/README.md)).
- **Verification contract** — the common output interface of all computational ports: banner → per-assertion PASS/FAIL lines → `JSON: {...}` verdict → exit code. The glue that makes eleven languages comparable.
- **Tier 1 / Tier 2** — the framework's two layers: proof assistants (formal) and computational languages (numerical), respectively.
- **Section (1–6)** — an atomic verification unit mapping one research claim onto one directory tree present in every language.

## 📐 Appendix A — Mathematical Background

This appendix collects the mathematical objects that recur across the repository, in one place, at the level of precision a working reader needs. It is a bridge text: enough formulas to make the verification contracts readable, and pointers to where each object is machine-checked or computed.

### A.1 The Kirchhoff System and the Birth of *b*

The point-vortex system of Kirchhoff evolves *N* point vortices at positions **x**ᵢ ∈ ℝ² with circulations Γᵢ:

- d**x**ᵢ/dt = (1/2π) Σⱼ≠ᵢ Γⱼ · (**x**ᵢ − **x**ⱼ)⊥ / |**x**ᵢ − **x**ⱼ|²

where ⊥ denotes rotation by −90°. The polarization of a vortex configuration — the orientation of its collective rotation plane in the 3D embedding — evolves under a twist operator whose fixed point yields the *polarization correction*:

- **b = π / (4π² + 2π√3) ≈ 0.0785**

Three derived facts (each a verified lemma in the formal tier):

1. **Positivity:** b > 0, since π > 0 and 4π² + 2π√3 > 0. (Lemmas `b_pos` in Lean 4 / Isabelle / Agda; `bCorrection_pos` in Coq.)
2. **Bound:** b < 1, because 4π² + 2π√3 > π. (Lemma `b_lt_one`.)
3. **Trigonometric identity:** sin θ_b = b with θ_b = arcsin(b) ≈ 7.07°. (Lemma `sin_θ_b_eq_b`.)

The BKM quantity controlled by the twist:

- ∫₀ᵀ ‖ω(·, t)‖_∞ dt < ∞ ⟹ no blow-up by time *T*,

and the polarization twist reduces this integral by a factor of **3.5×** in the corrected evolution — the numerical counterpart is asserted in every Section 1–2 port and the analytic skeleton is compiled in Lean 4 and Isabelle.

### A.2 The Hofstadter Skeleton

The Hofstadter Hamiltonian on a square lattice with flux α per plaquette:

- H_hof = Σ_{m,n} ( |m+1, n⟩⟨m, n| + |m, n+1⟩⟨m, n| + h.c. ) · phase(α; m, n)

with Peierls phases phase(α; m, n) = exp(2πi α m). Its spectrum as α ranges over rationals is the Hofstadter butterfly — fractal, with a self-similar gap structure. The repository verifies the structural properties of this object (flux quantisation, Hermiticity of the reference operator, symmetry classes) in Section 3 ports and in the formal tier.

**In 3D** the skeleton generalises to a cubic lattice with two additional hopping directions, carrying non-Hermitian terms — this is the frame of the AB-Cloud.

### A.3 The Aharonov–Bohm Decoration

An AB vortex threads flux Φ through a plaquette without touching the particle paths; it enters the Hamiltonian as a phase defect. With *Nᵥ* vortices on the 36³ cloud (σ = 0.5, α = 2.0, W = 1.0):

- H_abcloud = H_hof(3D) + Σ_{k=1..Nᵥ} U_k

where each U_k is a rank-local phase operator placed at the vortex position. The 5 000 embedded ζ-zeros fix the flux profile: their imaginary parts ζ(1/2 + iγₙ) parameterise the phase strengths — this is the *embedding* whose non-circularity is one of the three audited objections.

### A.4 The Statistical Toolkit — Exact Definitions

For an ordered eigenvalue sequence λ₁ ≤ λ₂ ≤ … of dimension *N*:

- **Nearest-neighbour spacings** sₙ = λₙ₊₁ − λₙ; unfolded spacing ~ uniform mean 1.
- **Gap ratio** rₙ = min(sₙ, sₙ₊₁) / max(sₙ, sₙ₊₁); the reported ⟨r⟩ = 0.6159 (36³ cloud) vs GUE 0.5996, GOE 0.5307, Poisson 0.3863.
- **Number variance** Σ²(L) = ⟨(N(L) − L)²⟩ over windows of length *L*.
- **Spectral rigidity** Δ₃(L) — the least-squares deviation of the staircase from the best linear fit on windows of length *L*.
- **Form factor** K(τ) — Fourier transform of the two-point cluster function; K(τ) = τ for τ < 1 in GUE.
- **KS statistic** D_N = supₓ |F_N(x) − F(x)|; reported as p-values 0.27–0.88 across sliding windows (cloud vs ζ).
- **Permutation test** — label-permutation null over surrogates; the observed statistic sits at **Z = 14.10σ**, p < 10⁻⁴⁴.

Every one of these has an implementation in the AB-Cloud suite (10 languages) and, where structural, a formal counterpart; the frozen ζ-zero table (`zeta_zeros_50000_embedded.txt`, 50 000 zeros) is the shared input everywhere.

### A.5 The Klein Quartic and Its 64 Structures

The Klein quartic:

- x³y + y³z + z³x = 0,

a genus-3 curve with automorphism group PSL(2,7) of order 168. Its Spin-liftings yield **64 inequivalent spinor structures** ε(1)…ε(64), organised into orbits **28 / 21 / 7 / 7 / 1** by the PSL(2,7) action. Each structure carries a quadratic form whose **Arf invariant** Arf(ε) ∈ Z/2 classifies the orbit membership.

The spinor64 programme tests each structure for GUE consistency of the associated Hofstadter torus spectrum (E2 sector, L = 44, α = 1/2, Nᵥ = 54):

- all 64 pass (Monte-Carlo min p = 0.36);
- exact isospectrality within an orbit: max|Δλ| = 8.9·10⁻¹⁵;
- gauge invariance: 7.1·10⁻¹⁵;
- zero-mode counts per orbit: 2 / 3 / 3 / 3 / 7;
- Arf(ε(38)) = 0 ⟹ the legacy «structure 38 is unique» claim is refuted.

The reference verification is [`ab-cloud/verification/spinor64/`](ab-cloud/verification/README.md); the Test-38 ports (`<lang>/spinor38/`) re-verify structure 64 (index 38) in each of 10 languages with a hand-written Jacobi eigensolver — deliberately library-free, so no LAPACK build can mask an error.

## 🌐 Appendix B — REST API Reference

The verification REST API ([`verification/api/server.py`](verification/api/)) exposes the section verifiers over HTTP, so external dashboards and CI jobs can trigger runs without cloning anything. All responses are JSON; all endpoints are read-only with respect to the repository.

**Start the server:**

```bash
pip install -r verification/api/requirements.txt
python verification/api/server.py            # default: http://127.0.0.1:5000
```

**Endpoints:**

| Method | Path | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `GET` | `/health` | liveness probe; returns `{"status": "ok", "version": …}` |
| `GET` | `/sections` | lists the six sections with their languages and expected value keys |
| `GET` | `/matrix` | the Section × Language matrix as JSON (same content as the README table) |
| `POST` | `/verify/<section>` | runs one section in one language; body `{"language": "python"\|"rust"\|…}`; returns the parsed JSON verdict and exit status |
| `POST` | `/verify_all` | runs a whole language chain; returns per-section verdicts |
| `GET` | `/history` | lists the locally archived run summaries, newest first |

**Response envelope for `/verify/<section>`:**

```json
{
  "section": 1,
  "language": "python",
  "exit_code": 0,
  "stdout_tail": "…[PASS] b_pos: expected=true, actual=true …",
  "verdict": {"section": 1, "language": "python", "values": {"b": 0.0785}, "all_passed": true}
}
```

The `verdict` object is exactly the JSON line the port prints — the API is a thin, faithful wrapper. A non-zero `exit_code` with `all_passed: false` means a genuine assertion mismatch, which is precisely what the cross-language validator treats as a failure.

**Rate & safety notes.** The server shells out to the local verifiers; it is intended for localhost / CI-side usage, not as a public service. Container deployments should expose it only on an internal network; the Docker images under [`verification/docker/`](verification/docker/README.md) are the reference environments for that.

**Interactive alternatives.** If a GUI is preferred over HTTP, the Gradio and Streamlit front-ends in [`verification/demo/`](verification/demo/README.md) wrap the same endpoints visually:

```bash
streamlit run verification/demo/streamlit_app.py
gradio verification/demo/gradio_app.py
```

## 🧩 Appendix D — Extending the Framework

Three worked checklists for the most common kinds of growth. Each keeps the invariant that matters most in this repository: **the cross-language validator must keep passing.**

### D.1 Add a New Language Port of an Existing Section

1. **Pick the layout** closest to your language: std-only → copy the Rust workspace package; matrix-heavy → the C++ harness; pure-functional → the Haskell package.
2. **Implement the contract exactly:** banner line → per-assertion `[PASS]/[FAIL]` lines → final `JSON: {"section": N, "language": "<lang>", "values": {…}, "all_passed": bool}` → exit code 0 only on full success. The JSON line is parsed mechanically; a missing line fails CI.
3. **Reuse frozen constants** — the expected values live in the section's README and in the Python reference; never re-derive them ad hoc inside the port.
4. **Register the port:** add a row to the [Section × Language matrix](#-section--language-verification-matrix), the language to [Language Profiles](#-language-profiles) if new, and the validator config in [`verification/tests/`](verification/tests/README.md).
5. **Document:** a README for the port directory (house style: purpose, files, commands, RU summary) and a one-line update here if the matrix changed.
6. **CI:** add the toolchain job to `ci-extended-languages.yml` (or open an issue asking for it) — a port that never runs in CI will rot silently.

### D.2 Add a New Section (Research Claim)

1. **Define the claim** so that its structural part is formalisable and its numeric part is computable from closed-form inputs (the section tier must not require datasets).
2. **Write the Python reference first** — it is the executable definition of the section; all other ports diff against it.
3. **Add formal counterparts** in as many assistants as the claim allows; list admitted gaps in `TODO_sorry.md` if any remain.
4. **Extend the matrix** — new row in the [matrix](#-section--language-verification-matrix), new directories per language, new validator entries.
5. **Wire documentation:** section README (with per-language commands), FAQ entry if the claim is headline-level, and a row in [Extended Quantitative Results](#-extended-quantitative-results) if it produces a headline number.

### D.3 Add a New Documentation Surface

1. Folder README first (house style), then the MkDocs nav in [`mkdocs.yml`](mkdocs.yml), then a row in the [Documentation](#-documentation) table if the surface is top-level.
2. Keep links relative and folder-local (`../sibling/README.md`) so the tree works on GitHub, in MkDocs and in the static site equally.
3. Badge hygiene: any new badge must be either dynamic-green or static-informational (see [Badge Health](#-badge-health--diagnostics)); test the URL in a incognito session before committing.

## ⏱ Appendix E — Performance Notes

Reference timings on a mid-range laptop (8 cores, 16 GB RAM, NVMe) — useful for budgeting CI time and for sanity-checking a local run that feels wrong.

## ❓ Appendix H — Extended FAQ

The FAQ in the main body answers the top questions; this appendix keeps the longer tail.

**Q: Is the correction *b* empirical or exact?**
Exact — it is a closed-form number π/(4π² + 2π√3) derived from the Kirchhoff system algebra. What is empirical in the vicinity is the *convergence law* of the Julia suite (b(N) ≈ 7.0312·N^(−0.1685)) — that one is a fit with bootstrap error bars and is reported as such (row 13 of the results table).

**Q: Why is the AB-Cloud Hamiltonian non-Hermitian if Hilbert–Pólya wants a self-adjoint operator?**
The non-Hermitian construction is the working stage of the programme; the correspondence is verified within a non-Hermitian→Hermitian-corrected framework — the corrected sector is where the GUE-class statistics are established. The monograph v22 devotes a chapter to this passage, and the structural lemmas of Section 3 verify the skeleton.

**Q: What exactly did the Test-38 re-verification change?**
One claim: uniqueness. v21 said structure 38 is the unique GUE-consistent spinor structure; the 10-language re-verification computed Arf(ε(38)) = 0, placing 38 in a known orbit class, and showed all 64 structures are GUE-consistent. Nothing else in v21 was touched — and the original v21 edition is preserved unmodified for transparency.

**Q: Why does the Python tier avoid NumPy in the six sections?**
Zero-dependency ports remove an entire class of supply-chain and version drift: the reference implementation is the *executable definition*, and every machine with Python 3.10+ can run it identically. NumPy enters in the AB-Cloud suites and the lab, where performance matters more than hermeticity.

**Q: How are the frozen ζ-zero tables produced?**
The 50 000-zero table was extracted verbatim from the embedded array of the historical Julia v19_v1 source (the extraction story is documented in `ab-cloud/code/julia/README.md`); the lab embeds 5 000 zeros. Both files are committed; nothing downloads at runtime.

**Q: Can the framework verify *my* claim?**
The framework is built for the six research sections, but the contract is generic. Appendix D.2 is the checklist for adding a section; the validator and the matrix make the addition mechanical. Fork, add, and keep the JSON verdict format.

**Q: Why does CI re-run proofs at all — isn't numeric verification enough?**
Numeric verification catches value drift; formal verification catches *statement* drift — a lemma that silently weakened. Running both tiers on every push is what lets the repository claim "nothing is hidden": any mismatch surfaces in the cheapest CI gate first.

**Q: Where is the line between this repository and the old standalone AB-Cloud repo?**
There is none anymore: since v1.5.0 the AB-Cloud complex lives here, consolidated at v1.2.0 (commit 32aa8e8), and the old scattered paths are retired. `ab-cloud/SNAPSHOT_INFO.md` is the authoritative mapping.

**Q: How do I get a JSON verdict for all sections at once, machine-readable?**
Either `make verify-all` (stdout carries the JSON lines) or the API's `POST /verify_all`; both return the same verdict objects, so scripts can treat them interchangeably.

**Q: Which language port should I trust most?**
The one you can read. The design assumption is that no single port is privileged: agreement across paradigms is the evidence. If you audit one, audit the pair (formal + computational) for your favourite section — the matrix shows the paths.

## 🌍 Appendix L — Language Deep Dives

For each of the eleven toolchains: what to install, how this repository builds with it, what the port layout looks like, and the one quirk most likely to bite. The per-language READMEs under [`verification/`](verification/README.md) remain the authoritative reference; this appendix is the quick orientation layer.

### L.1 Python (reference tier)

- **Install:** 3.10–3.12 via system, `pyenv` or conda (`conda env create -f environment.yml`).
- **Layout:** `verification/section{N}_<name>/python/verify.py` — one self-contained file per section; shared utilities in `verification/common/python/` (`verifier_base.py`, `config.py`).
- **Run:** any section directly with `python3 …`; all via `make verify-all`.
- **Quirk:** the six section ports deliberately import nothing beyond the standard library — if you see a third-party import, you are in the AB-Cloud suites, not the reference tier.

### L.2 Lean 4

- **Install:** `elan` (Lean version manager); toolchain pinned by `verification/lean4/lean-toolchain` (v4.14).
- **Layout:** `verification/lean4/ResearchPapersVerification/{Common,Section1…Section6}/` with `lakefile.lean` at the root; `TODO_sorry.md` itemises open branches.
- **Run:** `lake build` then `lake exe check` — the executable prints the same PASS/JSON contract as the computational tier.
- **Quirk:** the first build fetches Mathlib4 — use the cache (`lake exe cache get` where available) or expect a long compile; a toolchain mismatch triggers a full rebuild storm.

### L.3 Coq/Rocq

- **Install:** Coq 8.18 (opam: `opam install coq.8.18.0`).
- **Layout:** `verification/coq/section{N}_*/<Topic>.v` + `_CoqProject` listing the files; classical `Reals`-based proofs with `lra` automation.
- **Run:** `coqc` per file, in the order listed by `_CoqProject`.
- **Quirk:** `Reals` lemma names moved between Coq versions; with 8.16 or older you get unbound-identifier errors that look like proof bugs but are version skew.

### L.4 Isabelle-HOL

- **Install:** Isabelle 2024 with HOL image.
- **Layout:** `verification/isabelle/ROOT` (session) + `Section*/<Topic>.thy` over `Complex_Main`.
- **Run:** `isabelle build -D verification/isabelle` (or `isabelle jedit -l HOL verification/isabelle/Section1_CorrectionB/CorrectionB.thy` for interactive browsing).
- **Quirk:** build per session, not per file — a per-file invocation skips the `ROOT`-managed imports and fails confusingly on the first build.

### L.5 Agda

- **Install:** Agda 2.6 + standard library per `verification/agda/agda.agda-lib`.
- **Layout:** `verification/agda/Section*/<Topic>.agda`; π and √3 are explicit postulates — the trust base is documented in each file header.
- **Run:** `agda <file>.agda` from within the `verification/agda/` directory (so the `.agda-lib` resolves).
- **Quirk:** running from the repo root without the library file yields unresolved include paths; the error is about modules, not mathematics.

### L.6 Rust

- **Install:** rustup (stable ≥ 1.75).
- **Layout:** `verification/rust/` — one Cargo workspace, six packages (`section1_correction_b` … `section6_riemann_zeros`), std-only (no external crates).
- **Run:** `cargo run --release -p <package>` or the whole chain without `-p`.
- **Quirk:** debug builds are noticeably slow for the larger sections; `--release` is the supported mode everywhere in the docs.

### L.7 C++

- **Install:** CMake ≥ 3.28, a C++17 compiler, BLAS/LAPACK (OpenBLAS recommended).
- **Layout:** `verification/cpp/CMakeLists.txt` builds six targets; per-section sources in `verification/cpp/section*/main.cpp`.
- **Run:** `cmake -S verification/cpp -B build && cmake --build build` then per-target executables from `build/`.
- **Quirk:** CMake prints the BLAS provider it found; "found nothing" usually means the dev packages (not just runtime libs) are missing.

### L.8 Haskell

- **Install:** GHC 9.4 via ghcup; Cabal.
- **Layout:** `verification/haskell/` — six `.cabal` packages + `cabal.project`; pure implementations of the section contracts.
- **Run:** `cabal build` once at the root, then `cabal run <section-package>`.
- **Quirk:** the first `cabal update` matters; a stale package index produces resolver errors unrelated to the code.

### L.9 Julia (AB-Cloud tier)

- **Install:** Julia 1.10+ (the reference runs used 1.12.0).
- **Layout:** `ab-cloud/code/julia/` — the provenance archive (v19_v1, v20, v21, v21_v1) plus the current suite; run scripts and manifests per folder.
- **Run:** per the runners in [`ab-cloud/code/julia/README.md`](ab-cloud/code/julia/README.md); the full two-pass suite takes hours (archived reference: 37 tests, 72×72 → 96×96).
- **Quirk:** the embedded zero tables are part of the source (that is the point — frozen data); do not "optimise" them out, the provenance contract depends on them.

### L.10 JavaScript / React (web apps)

- **Install:** Node 18+ (LTS).
- **Layout:** `ab-cloud/apps/ab-cloud-dashboard/` and `ab-cloud/apps/ab-cloud-lab3d/` — Vite + React apps; the dashboard computes statistics in a web worker over the embedded 50 000-zero table; lab3d renders Three.js scenes.
- **Run:** `npm install && npm run dev` per app; production bundles are committed under `dist/`.
- **Quirk:** the committed `dist/` folders are intentionally present (no-build serving); a clean rebuild regenerates them with `npm run build`.

### L.11 The Extended Ring (Go · Fortran · R · MATLAB)

- **Where:** `ab-cloud/verification/<lang>/` — one folder per language with its own runner (`run_verify.sh` / `run_verify.py`).
- **Run:** e.g. `bash ab-cloud/verification/cpp/run_verify.sh`, or the language-idiomatic runner of each folder.
- **Purpose:** each of the three reviewer objections is answered in the language the reviewer prefers; the ring exists so no one has to take Python's word for anything.
- **Quirk:** MATLAB scripts are reference-checked against Octave-compatible syntax where possible, but the reference runs are MATLAB-made; CI covers the open-source members of the ring.

## 🎓 Appendix W — Formal Verification Deep Dive

What the four proof assistants actually carry, stated precisely enough that a logician can audit the trust base from this page alone.

### W.1 The Lean 4 Foundation

The shared basis lives in [`verification/lean4/ResearchPapersVerification/Common/Foundation.lean`](verification/lean4/ResearchPapersVerification/Common/Foundation.lean) — one definition and two closed theorems set the pattern for everything else:

```lean
def bCorrection : ℝ := Real.pi / (4 * Real.pi^2 + 2 * Real.pi * Real.sqrt 3)

theorem bCorrection_pos : 0 < bCorrection := by
  unfold bCorrection
  have h1 : (0:ℝ) < 4 * Real.pi^2 := by nlinarith [Real.pi_pos]
  have h2 : (0:ℝ) < 2 * Real.pi * Real.sqrt 3 := by positivity
  exact div_pos Real.pi_pos (by linarith)
```

On top of the foundation, Section 1 develops the geometric core: the Rodrigues rotation constructor, the unit axis **e**_Z, the cross-product matrix, and the corrected rotation R_b with its two load-bearing properties:

- `R_b_orthogonal : R_bᵀ * R_b = 1` — the twist is a genuine rotation;
- `R_b_det_one : R_b.det = 1` — orientation-preserving, no reflection sneaks in.

The trigonometric bridge is the identity `sin_θ_b_eq_b : Real.sin θ_b = bCorrection`, proved from `Real.sin_arcsin` plus the two foundation theorems — exactly the statement the computational ports assert numerically.

### W.2 The Honest Gap Ledger

[`verification/lean4/TODO_sorry.md`](verification/lean4/TODO_sorry.md) itemises every admitted gap in the Lean development — **13 open `sorry`s** (numerical bound lemmas such as `b_gt_007`, `b_lt_008` and the orthogonality/determinant generalities above), **12 `axiom … : True` stubs** awaiting replacement by `theorem … := trivial`, and **4 genuine open-problem axioms** that stay visible rather than pretending to be proved. The ledger is the opposite of a hidden weakness: it is the work plan, with per-item difficulty estimates, and the corresponding gaps in Coq/Isabelle/Agda are cross-referenced in their per-language READMEs.

### W.3 The Same Content in Three Other Systems

| Assistant | Section 1 file | Style | Trust base |
|---|---|---|---|
| Coq/Rocq | `section1_correction_b/CorrectionB.v` | classical `Reals` + `lra`/`lia` automation | Coq kernel, standard library |
| Isabelle-HOL | `Section1_CorrectionB/CorrectionB.thy` | Isar structured proofs over `Complex_Main` | HOL kernel, ROOT session |
| Agda | `Section1_CorrectionB/CorrectionB.agda` | dependent types, constructive | π and √3 as explicit postulates — nothing else |

A reviewer diffing the four developments sees the *same* lemma names modulo naming conventions (`b_pos` / `bCorrection_pos` / `b-pos`), the same proof obligations, and four independent kernels checking them. That redundancy — four kernels, seven numeric ecosystems — is the strongest guarantee the framework offers.

### W.4 What Formal Verification Does *Not* Claim

No formal claim in this repository says "the 3D Navier–Stokes equations are regular" as a compiled theorem — the heavy analytic steps live in the papers, not in a proof assistant. The formal tier verifies the *structural skeleton* the argument stands on (the constant's properties, the rotation algebra, the matrix structures, the Hofstadter identities). Papers and proofs are kept deliberately separate, and every results-table row says which side of that line it lives on.
